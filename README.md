## About

Public corruption — the abuse of public office for private gain — undermines democratic institutions and erodes public trust. The FBI Public Corruption program and DOJ Public Integrity Section prioritize investigating and prosecuting corruption at all levels of government.

## Federal Corruption Laws

### 18 U.S.C. § 201 — Bribery of Public Officials
Covers bribery of federal officials. Requires a "quid pro quo" — an exchange of something of value for an official act.

### 18 U.S.C. § 666 — Theft or Bribery Concerning Programs Receiving Federal Funds
Extends federal corruption jurisdiction to state and local officials whose agencies receive federal funding. Most state and local government receives some federal funding, making this statute extremely broad.

### 18 U.S.C. § 1951 — Hobbs Act Extortion
Covers extortion under color of official right — officials demanding payments in exchange for official actions. Does not require proof of explicit quid pro quo.

### 18 U.S.C. § 1341/1343 — Mail/Wire Fraud
The honest services fraud theory (18 U.S.C. § 1346) allows prosecution of officials who deprive the public of honest services — limiting this to bribery and kickback schemes after Supreme Court decisions.

## Types of Public Corruption Cases

### Elected Official Corruption
Federal prosecution of mayors, city council members, state legislators, congressional members, and other elected officials for bribery, kickbacks, and fraud.

### Law Enforcement Corruption
Police officers and sheriffs accepting bribes to protect drug operations, stealing from crime scenes, or violating civil rights. DOJ Civil Rights Division and FBI jointly investigate.

### Judicial Corruption
Bribery of judges or court officials — rare but seriously prosecuted.

### Procurement Fraud
Government contractors and officials conspiring to rig bids, create fraudulent contracts, and steal from government programs.

## FBI Public Corruption Program

The FBI reports that it opens approximately 2,500+ public corruption investigations annually. The program has over 1,000 agents working on corruption cases. Corruption is considered a "tier 1" FBI priority alongside terrorism and counterintelligence.
